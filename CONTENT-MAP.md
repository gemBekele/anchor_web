# Content map — Anchor Financials blog

Source: **The Cooperative Ledger — Ethiopia's Last Undigitised Frontier** (Issue 01, 25 September 2026,
Anchor Financials Research Desk), plus the underlying `market_data.csv` (93 metrics) and
`ethiopia_sacco_prospects.csv` (154 organisations) referenced by that report.

## Editorial rules applied

- Every figure traces to a named source in the report. Nothing inferred, nothing invented.
- Derived figures are labelled as derived. The report's own caveats are carried into the articles
  rather than smoothed over.
- Where the report says "unknown" or "not published", the article says so too.
- Prices in the market model are Anchor planning assumptions, not vendor quotes — stated as such.
- The market-sizing and prospect-database material is Anchor's own analysis; articles using it say
  "our research" or attribute it, rather than implying it is external research.

## Fact bank (verified against source)

**Sector shape**
23,659 primary SACCOs (ECC via ENA, Jun 2025) · 125 unions · 2 federations (2022) · 8.3M members,
48% women (Jun 2025) · 79% rural / 21% urban · ~2% of the credit market (2023).
Growth: 495 cooperatives / <120k members (1991) → 14,453 / 1.7M (2014) → 21,328 / 5.4M (2021) →
23,659 / 8.3M (2025).
Definitions conflict: ECC 23,659 vs WOCCU 18,254 vs NBE "over 21,000". Not additive.
Savings 2022: Addis Ababa 13.5bn · Oromia 9.4bn · Amhara 5.2bn birr (only three published).
Amhara: 2,982 SACCOs, 97 unions, 1 federation, >1M members (2023).
Derived: 351 members/cooperative · ~6,250 birr deposits per member.

**Money**
SACCO deposits >ETB 50bn (2023) · loans disbursed >ETB 15bn (2023) · capital >ETB 21bn (2021).
MFIs FY2024/25: 56–62 licensed · assets 81.7bn (+35.9%) · deposits 41.78bn · gross loans 48.95bn ·
capital 15.95bn · 1,238 branches · 752,387 loan clients · 7,109,133 savers · CAR 30.3% (min 12%) ·
NPL 3.3% · 1.5% of system assets (banks 87.5%).
Five largest MFIs became banks 2019–2023 (Tsedey, Siinqee, Omo, Shabelle, Sidama).
Dedebit (DECSI) ~20% of remaining assets. VisionFund US$10m IFC loan (Sep 2025).

**Digitisation**
<1% (well under 100 named deployments) · 2023: Awach described as "the only cooperative that installs
the core banking system" · Coopbank SACCO-Link 42 SACCOs + 10 unions (2024) → 55 (2025) · Kefeta
digitised 18 youth cooperatives · ~4 confirmed public mobile apps (Amigos, Yehulu, Sheger, Horizon).
Four barriers: cost, connectivity/power, skills, opacity.

**Infrastructure**
Telebirr 60.59M customers, ETB 4.19tn (FY2025/26, ~20% of GDP) · M-Pesa 5.2M · 136M mobile-money
accounts, ~15% active · Fayda 49.1M enrolments (Aug 2026) · EthSwitch 287.4M transactions,
ETB 741.1bn (FY2024/25) · EthioPay launched Dec 2025 · internet 21.7% · rural electricity 43.6% ·
smartphone costs 77% of monthly income · account ownership 48.8% (2024) vs 21.8% (2014).

**Policy**
NDPS 2026–2030 (Dec 2025): digital payments 82% → 750% of GDP by 2030; proposes a shared core-banking
platform for SACCOs · NBE to supervise SACCOs and pension funds (Mar 2026, with ECC) · MFI minimum
capital ETB 10m → 75m, comply by **Jan 2028** · MFI/33/2022 IT-governance directive ·
Proclamation 985/2016, Directive 982/2016 · Digital Ethiopia 2030.

**Funding**
>US$1.5bn development finance. IFAD RUFIP III US$305.7m (via DBE) · RUFIP II US$169.51m actual ·
EIB EUR 110m (Feb 2026) + EUR 8.5m TA · World Bank PCDP III US$210.2m (~1,110 pastoral SACCOs) ·
DRDIP II ~US$280m · DRIVE US$360.5m · USAID Kefeta US$60m (US$500k Awash Bank for fintech) ·
RIPA-North US$45m · Mastercard/SAFEE US$100m unlocking US$300m (Kifiya, AI scoring) · Mastercard
Ethiopia portfolio US$672m · GIZ SRVC II EUR 15.525m · ATI ETB 42m IT equipment (Tigray, Dec 2024) ·
AEMFI shared core US$3m, 40 MFIs, 1.28m clients (Craft Silicon).

**Competition / pricing**
Coopbank SACCO-Link · Ethio Telecom cloud core (Jun 2025) · AEMFI shared core · Pitron FinOS (Jul 2026,
Temenos partner) · Seqela (AI scoring) · Techverve SaccoVerve (live at Tederash) · Ashewa (Mifos X).
Published prices: Kwara from 30,000 KES/month (~USD 230) · Musoni Starter 999 EUR/month (≤3,000 clients)
· Loan Performer USD 1,107 first year, then 207/yr · Mifos X 0 USD licence. **Ethiopian vendors
publish nothing.**

**Prospect database**
154 organisations (126 cooperatives, 28 unions/federations) · 31 with a named decision-maker ·
26 strong buying signals · 104 none found · priorities HIGH 27, MEDIUM-HIGH 16, MEDIUM 50,
LOW-MEDIUM 9, LOW 111 · regions Oromia 82, Addis Ababa 76, South Ethiopia 19, Amhara 13, Tigray 7.
Gap: Somali, Afar, Gambella not verifiable from public sources.

**Known unknowns (must be stated, not guessed)**

## What this source cannot support

If you want more than 22, these are the gaps — each would unlock roughly 3–5 further articles:

1. **Tender data.** No agency publishes cooperative software tender values, awards or losing bidders.
   Award data would support a series on public procurement.
2. **Named cooperative interviews.** Only 31 of 154 organisations have a publicly named decision-maker,
   and the report holds no verified quotes. First-hand interviews would unlock 6–8 practitioner pieces
   and real case studies.
3. **Regional breakdown after 2022.** Only three regions have published savings figures. A full
   regional dataset would support one article per major region.
4. **Somali, Afar and Gambella.** Nothing verifiable from public sources; these need field or
   regulator outreach.
5. **Pricing.** Only three African vendors publish prices. Real per-member price points for Ethiopian
   cooperatives would strengthen every article in group B.
